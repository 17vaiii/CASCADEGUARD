# Lessons from the 14 August 2003 North American blackout

## What happened
On 14 August 2003 a cascading outage in the north-eastern United States and Ontario interrupted supply to about 50 million people. The joint U.S.-Canada task force report traced the start to a sequence of transmission line trips in Ohio after lines sagged into trees, while the local control room had lost its alarm functionality and did not recognise the deteriorating situation.

## Lessons relevant to decision support
The report stressed situational awareness: operators need real-time tools that show the effect of an outage on the remaining lines, and clear guidance on corrective action. Line overloads that are not relieved within minutes can lead to further trips and, ultimately, to uncontrolled separation of the network. Timely, coordinated load shedding can limit the extent of a cascade.
