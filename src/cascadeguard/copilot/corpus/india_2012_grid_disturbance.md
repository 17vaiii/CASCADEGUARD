# Lessons from the July 2012 grid disturbances in India

## What happened
On 30 and 31 July 2012 major grid disturbances affected the Northern, Eastern and North-Eastern regional grids of India; the second event is widely reported as affecting around 600 million people. Public enquiry reports describe a weakened inter-regional transmission network carrying heavy power flows, with the first event starting from the tripping of the 400 kV Bina-Gwalior line, followed by cascading line trips and loss of synchronism.

## Lessons relevant to decision support
The findings highlight the risk of operating heavily loaded corridors with reduced redundancy, the need to respect scheduled interchange and to act on warnings, and the value of coordinated defence plans, including load shedding, that act before overloads spread. Better wide-area visibility of corridor loading was a recurring recommendation.
