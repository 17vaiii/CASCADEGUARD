to Bengaluru power corridor
Author: Vaibhav | Status: Draft v1 | Based on: CascadeGuard v2 project report | SDG 9
Note on facts: Plant capacities, line voltages, substation names and loss figures
below are working assumptions from public knowledge. They must be verified
against KPCL, KPTCL, SLDC Karnataka and BESCOM published data before being
used in the report. All results in the student version come from simulation and

1. Summary
CascadeGuard v2 is a generic pipeline: screen, predict, explain, retrieve similar cases,
recommend the smallest fix, verify, brief the operator. This PRD adapts it to one real-
world corridor: hydro generation in the Sharavathi valley (Linganamakki reservoir,
Sharavathi and Mahatma Gandhi power stations near Jog, Gerusoppa and related
stations), transmitted through Shivamogga district to the Bengaluru load centre.
It adds a second capability that the generic version does not have: electricity theft and
non-technical loss detection, using energy-balance analysis and smart-meter style data
on the Bengaluru side of the corridor.
The product has two pillars sharing one data and graph backbone:
1. Grid resilience: predict, explain and mitigate cascading failures on the corridor.
2. Loss and theft intelligence: locate where energy is being lost or stolen, and separate it
from normal technical loss.
2. Problem Statement
Resilience. Bengaluru depends on a small number of long transmission paths from hydro
generation in the Western Ghats, plus other sources. Losing one or two corridor elements
shifts flow onto neighbours and can start a cascade. Operators rely on N-1 studies, which
are slow for N-2 and N-3 and give no explanation or recommended action. Hydro adds its
own complexity: output depends on reservoir level and monsoon inflow, so the operating
point changes season to season.

Page 1 of 24\n--- PAGE 2 ---\nTheft. A meaningful part of energy losses in distribution is non-technical: meter
tampering, direct hooking, billing errors and illegal tapping of HT lines. Today it is found by
inspection drives and periodic audits, which are reactive and expensive. Technical loss
(heating in lines and transformers) is physics. Non-technical loss is behaviour. Mixing
them hides the real problem.
Gap this product fills:
Fast screening of thousands of failure combinations on this specific corridor, with
explanations and verified minimal fixes.
A unified energy-balance view that quantifies expected technical loss from the power-
flow model and flags the unexplained remainder as suspected theft, by feeder and by
consumer cluster.
One platform for control-room, planning and vigilance teams instead of separate tools.
3. Goals and Non-Goals
Goals
G1. Predict cascade extent and screen N-2/N-3 on a model of the Sharavathi to
Bengaluru corridor much faster than full power-flow, with calibrated uncertainty.
G2. Explain critical lines, likely cascade path and give a verified minimal mitigation.
G3. Model hydro-specific behaviour: seasonal inflow, reservoir-constrained dispatch,
and fast hydro ramping as a mitigation option.
G4. Detect suspected theft at feeder and consumer-cluster level with a transparent,
explainable score.
G5. Rank theft leads for field inspection so that limited inspection teams visit the
highest-yield locations first.
G6. Run as a deployable, cloud-served system with a live dashboard on simulated
telemetry.
Non-Goals
Not a control system. It advises; humans decide. It never sends commands to real
equipment.
No use of real consumer personal data in the student version. Synthetic and public
datasets only.
Not accusing individuals. Theft output is a ranked lead for inspection, never a verdict.
No claims of real-world accuracy until validated on utility data.

5. System Context and Scope
Corridor modelled (assumption, to be verified):
Generation: Sharavathi valley hydro stations as aggregated or per-unit generator
buses, with reservoir-linked energy limits.
Transmission: high-voltage lines and substations through Shivamogga district towards
Bengaluru, with interconnections to the wider Karnataka grid represented as
equivalent buses.
Load: Bengaluru as a set of load buses (substation level), with feeder-level detail for
the theft module.
Grid model strategy. Because the real network model is not public, build in layers:
1. Start with IEEE 14/39/118 as in v2, for method validation and generalisation tests.
2. Build a reduced Karnataka corridor model (roughly 30 to 60 buses) from public single-
line information, with clearly documented assumptions.
3. Add synthetic topologies shaped like a long radial-to-meshed hydro corridor, for
generalisation.
4. If a utility ever shares data, swap in the real model without changing the pipeline.

7. Theft Detection: Detailed Design
7.1 Approach
Theft is hard to label because most of it is never caught. So the design avoids depending
on real labels.
1. Physics first. The grid model gives expected technical loss. Whatever remains
unexplained at a feeder is the candidate non-technical loss.
2. Behaviour second. Consumer-level models look for patterns that deviate from the
consumer's own history and from similar peers.
3. Fusion. A feeder with a high residual raises the prior for every consumer below it. A
consumer with a strong anomaly on a high-residual feeder gets the highest score.
7.2 Data for the student version
Synthetic generator: simulate consumers by type (residential, commercial, small
industrial) with daily and seasonal patterns, then inject theft types with known labels:
meter bypass (constant fractional reduction), meter stop, night-time tapping, hooking
(unmetered extra load on a feeder), and temporary tampering.
Public datasets: use open smart-meter datasets for realistic base load behaviour, and
a public electricity-theft benchmark dataset for supervised comparison. Choose and
cite exact datasets during Phase 1.

Scores are leads, not accusations. Require human inspection before any action.
Keep false-positive rate visible; optimise for precision at top-K because inspections
are costly and wrongful flags harm consumers.
Check for bias by area or consumer category, and report it.

Page 8 of 24\n--- PAGE 9 ---\n8. Architecture
Flow: corridor grid model and synthetic meter data → cloud scenario and data generator
(Ray, Parquet on S3/GCS) → graph builder → GNN models (cascade and theft) with
calibration → screening, explainability, case retrieval → OR-Tools mitigation and simulator
verification → LLM brief → FastAPI service → Streamlit dashboard, with streaming
telemetry feeding live scoring.
Role separation (unchanged from v2): the GNN predicts, OR-Tools decides, the simulator
verifies, the LLM only explains. For theft: the models score, the physics model constrains,
the human inspects.
Cloud: same justification as v2 (embarrassingly parallel scenario and meter-data
generation, one cloud GPU for training, containerised serving) with a hard cost cap using
spot instances and auto-shutdown.
9. Non-Functional Requirements
Latency: single-scenario prediction under 1 second end to end via API; streaming alert
under 1 second from snapshot to alert.
Scalability: report scenarios per hour on the cluster versus a single machine; theft
scoring must handle at least hundreds of thousands of synthetic consumers in batch.
Reproducibility: fixed seeds, versioned datasets and tracked experiments.
Safety: advisory only, with no write path to any control system.
Explainability: every alert or lead has human-readable reasons.
Privacy: no real personal data in the student build; identifiers hashed in demos.
Cost: total cloud spend within a stated student budget.

14. Expected Final Demo
1. Dashboard shows the Sharavathi to Bengaluru corridor, all lines normal, telemetry
streaming, season selector set to monsoon.
2. Trip a corridor line. The system shows predicted cascade, calibrated probabilities,
critical lines, path and similar past cases.
3. It recommends a minimal action, possibly including hydro ramp-up within reservoir
limits, with a verification tick. Apply it: load loss drops (e.g., 18% to 4%).
4. Switch to the Loss and Theft view: feeder heatmap, technical vs non-technical loss
split, and a ranked inspection list with reasons.
5. Show the link: with suspected hidden load included, the cascade prediction for the
same trip changes, demonstrating why theft detection improves grid safety.
6. Show the results table for both pillars, ablations and cost summary.
15. Research Novelty
Existing work treats cascade prediction and theft detection as separate problems.
This product connects them: unmetered load biases the grid state and raises cascade
risk.

Page 23 of 24\n--- PAGE 24 ---\n17.3 Handoff rules
A handoff is complete only when the receiver can run it from the repo without asking
the sender anything.
Each handoff comes with: the code, a three-line readme, a sample input and output in
the agreed schema, and the tracked experiment link.
If a handoff is more than two days late, the receiver starts with a mock version in the
agreed format, so nobody sits idle.
Anything that changes a shared contract needs agreement from all five and a note in
the repo.
