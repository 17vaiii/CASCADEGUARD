## 2. Design decisions

1. **P4-side distribution simulator** (`feeder_sim.py`). P2's `meter_generator.py` does not keep
   each consumer's *true* demand, so a feeder-head meter cannot be derived from it. P4 simulates
   corridor substation → 11 kV feeder → DT → consumer, with true and metered energy, hidden load,
   feeder/DT input meters, tamper logs and coordinates. Meter rows follow the frozen P2 contract
   (`DistributionDataset.to_meter_frame()`), so the consumer models also run on P2 data.
2. **Hard negatives, so results are not inflated** (PRD risk "synthetic theft too easy"):
   theft clustered on hotspot feeders; rooftop solar, new appliance and vacancy look-alikes;
   holidays; meter communication gaps (NaN reads); legitimate unbilled loads (streetlights,
   late-billed new connections); feeder/DT meter bias, drift and noise; spurious tamper alarms
   (solar export triggers reverse-current events).
3. **Physics first (T1).** Each feeder is a pandapower 11 kV model. A loss curve
   `a + bP + cP²` (relative-error weighted) is fitted from pandapower runs; ground-truth losses use
   per-DT curves on actual DT loads. The estimator only sees the measured feeder input.
4. **Residual *change*, not level (T2).** Every feeder carries 1.5–2 % unexplained loss even
   without theft (streetlights, meter bias). The signal is the 7-day-peak change of the residual
   against the node's own reference period, as a z-score.
5. **Two kinds of lead (T6).** Hooking and night tapping never show on any consumer meter, so
   consumer models cannot find them. A DT whose residual is not explained by consumer leads becomes
   a network lead ("patrol for hooks / direct taps").
6. **Evaluation.** The fixed consumer-level train/validation/test split follows the P2 contract.
   For whole-population inspection and fairness, scores are **cross-fitted** (5 folds), so every
   consumer gets an out-of-fold score.
7. **T9 uses the P1 simulator as ground truth and as the predictor.** The P3 GNN is queried too;
   see the finding in §4.
8. **No new dependencies:** numpy, pandas, scikit-learn, scipy, pandapower, torch,
   torch-geometric, networkx, joblib, pyyaml.

## 4. Verified results (full run, seed 42, scale 2.0, 2026-11-05, 247 s on CPU)

Data: 48 feeders, 264 DTs (163 metered), **5,281 consumers**, 90 days hourly (45 reference +
45 scoring), 264 injected thefts (≈53 per type), 528 look-alikes, test split 792 consumers
(40 thefts). K = inspection capacity = 40.

**T1/T2**
- Corridor transmission loss (P1 model, nominal): 13.0 MW = 3.49 % of generation.
- Feeder loss-curve fit: max relative error 0.2 % against pandapower.
- Distribution technical loss, estimated vs true: 2.68 % vs 2.69 % of feeder input (error −0.01 pp).
- Unexplained residual: 2.00 % in the reference period (no theft; streetlights + meter bias),
  3.22 % in the scoring window; true NTL 1.24 %.
- Feeder localisation: 5 of the top 5 feeders by z contain theft; 26 flagged at z ≥ 3, all
  correct (precision 1.0, recall 0.62); Spearman(z, true NTL) = 0.90.
- DT localisation: flag precision 0.90, recall 0.83.
- NTL ratio per substation (T9 input), estimated vs true: PEENYA 1.65 vs 1.63 %, SHIMOGA 1.24 vs
  1.26 %, SIRA 1.51 vs 1.73 %, NELAMANGALA 0.67 vs 0.65 %.

**T3/T4/P4-6, held-out test split** (40 thefts in 792 consumers)

| Model | AUROC | PR-AUC | Precision@40 (= R-precision) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Threshold baseline (drop rule) | 0.787 | 0.388 | 0.325 |
| Tamper events only | 0.665 | 0.367 | 0.375 |
| Isolation Forest | 0.718 | 0.293 | 0.225 |
| Autoencoder | 0.740 | 0.405 | 0.425 |
| Peer deviation | 0.781 | 0.371 | 0.375 |
| Feeder residual only | 0.776 | 0.110 | 0.100 |
| Gradient boosting | 0.917 | 0.711 | 0.650 |
| GraphSAGE (P4-6) | 0.902 | 0.674 | 0.625 |
| **Fusion (shipped)** | **0.919** | **0.716** | **0.650** |

Random precision = 0.05. Recall@40 by type (fusion): meter bypass 1.00, meter stop 1.00,
temporary tampering 1.00, hooking 0.12, night tapping 0.12. Hooking and night tapping leave
consumer meters unchanged, so they are found through network leads (T6), not consumer scores.
The graph model did not beat fusion, so fusion ships; this negative result is reported.

Cross-fitted on all 5,281 consumers: fusion AUROC 0.887, PR-AUC 0.681, R-precision 0.617
(threshold baseline 0.770 / 0.385 / 0.333), precision@40 = 1.00.

**Calibration:** temperature T = 0.91; ECE 0.012 → **0.0055** after scaling (raw GBM 0.021);
cross-fitted ECE 0.0075. Conformal α = 0.10: test coverage **0.886**; confidence mix 13 high,
78 medium, 701 low. Top GBM permutation importances: `f_min7_ratio`, `f_dt_z`,
`f_dt_change_pct`, `f_feeder_change_pct`, `f_solar_signature`.

**T6 inspection** (40 visits, simulated truth, cross-fitted scores)

| Strategy | Hits | Precision | Network leads | Recovered kWh | kWh / visit | Travel km | Net value (INR) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random (mean of 20) | 1.4 | 0.04 | 0.9 | 700 | 18 | 115 | −13,976 |
| Rule-based (largest drop) | 40 | 1.00 | 0 | 48,997 | 1,225 | 111 | 324,206 |
| Score only | 40 | 1.00 | 0 | 24,284 | 607 | 122 | 150,948 |
| Expected value | 35 | 0.88 | 13 | 105,926 | 2,648 | 112 | 722,678 |
| **Expected value + routes** | 35 | 0.88 | 13 | 105,869 | 2,647 | **110** | 722,330 |

Ranking by expected recovered energy recovers **2.2×** the rule-based energy and **151×** random,
because it also sends crews to DTs with unmetered (hooking / tapping) loss. The routed variant
saves 2 km of travel; the value gain comes from the lead choice, not the routing.
Lead list: 35 of 40 hits (27 consumer and 13 network leads).

**T10 retrieval:** for test-set thefts, 48 % of the 5 nearest past cases are thefts and 38 % share
the exact outcome type (6 outcome classes).

**P4-11 fairness** (cross-fitted, threshold 0.5): overall FPR 0.10 %. FPR by area: peri-urban
0.05 %, rural 0.16 %, urban 0.13 % (disparity 3.2×). By type: residential 0.03 %, commercial
0.36 %, small industrial 0.28 % (disparity 13.8×). The absolute rates are tiny but uneven:
commercial and industrial consumers carry most false flags. At top-40 there were no false
positives. Flag-rate disparity at top-K: area 2.6×, type 1.9×.

**T9 theft-aware cascade** (196 contingencies per season: 76 N-1 + 120 sampled N-2; hidden load =
simulated NTL ratio × intensity, 4–28 MW). A failed AC solve counts as a voltage collapse with all
load lost (fixed 2026-11-07; before that it read as 0 MW, understating summer ×5).

| Monsoon | ×1 | 31 | 31 | 0 | 49.1 → 0.01 | 47.6 → 31.9 |
| Monsoon | ×3 | 81 | 81 | 0 | 131.7 → 0.05 | 130.2 → 2.8 |
| Monsoon | ×5 | 81 | 81 | 0 | 135.1 → 0.07 | 133.6 → 4.1 |
| Post-monsoon | ×1 | 81 | 0 | 0 | 1.9 → 0.02 | 1.6 → 2.8 |
| Post-monsoon | ×3 | 81 | 0 | 0 | 5.8 → 0.05 | 4.2 → 2.4 |
| Post-monsoon | ×5 | 151 | 70 | 0 | 118.5 → 0.12 | 116.8 → 14.7 |

Monsoon N-1 post-trip loading sits at about 99 %, so **4 MW of hidden load turns 31 "safe"
contingencies into cascades that SCADA-based screening cannot see.** P4's estimated hidden load
recovers all of them.

**GNN:** P3's checkpoint gave flat predictions on root-corridor snapshots, so `theft/root_gnn.py`
trains P3's unchanged `train_pipeline` on root P1 scenarios with varied hidden load (5,512 scenarios:
3,858 train, 826 validation, 828 test). Test AUROC 0.9996, F1 0.986, load-loss MAE 6.0 MW
(Random Forest 0.82, 0.69, 108 MW), calibrated ECE 0.012, conformal coverage 90.1 %. It is written to
`data/models/root_gnn/`; P3's `data/models/cascade_gnn.pt` is untouched. Where hidden load decides the
outcome (monsoon, post-monsoon ×5) the GNN gains a lot; where it does not, its error is slightly worse
with the added load (summer, post-monsoon ×1). The test split shares operating states with training,
so the T9 table above, on unseen hidden-load shapes, is the fairer view.

**T8 HT tapping** (300 cases, half with a 2–30 MW tap at a random 10 km tap bus, post-monsoon):
false alarms 0 %, recall 56 % overall (15–30 MW: 83 %, 5–15 MW: 39 %, 2–5 MW: 0 %), median
location error 20 km, magnitude MAE 2.6 MW. The ±10 % uncertainty of the unmetered SIRA_KBCROSS
load looks like a ~4 MW tap, which sets the detection floor.

```bash

PYTHONPATH=src .venv/Scripts/python -W ignore -m cascadeguard.theft.pipeline
#   --quick (scale 0.5, no T9/T8, ~80 s) | --scale 2.0 | --skip-t9 | --skip-t8 | --jobs 4 | --track (MLflow)

PYTHONPATH=src .venv/Scripts/python -m pytest tests/theft -q -W ignore      # 29 tests

# MLflow: set MLFLOW_TRACKING_URI=sqlite:///experiments/mlflow.db and add --track
# API (after the pipeline has written the bundle)
PYTHONPATH=src .venv/Scripts/python -m uvicorn cascadeguard.api.main:app --port 8000
```
PowerShell: `$env:PYTHONPATH="src"; .\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m cascadeguard.theft.pipeline`.

`POST /api/theft-score` body:
```json
{"snapshot": {"readings": [{"timestamp": "2025-04-20T10:00:00Z", "consumer_id": "C0000001",
               "feeder_id": "PEENYA_F00", "consumer_type": "residential", "metered_kwh": 0.42}],
              "history_end": "2025-04-15T00:00:00Z",
              "tamper_events": [{"consumer_id": "C0000001", "timestamp": "...", "event_type": "cover_open"}],
              "feeder_inputs": [{"timestamp": "...", "feeder_id": "PEENYA_F00", "input_kwh": 120.5}]},
 "options": {"top_k": 20}}
```
The response holds ranked `leads` (score, confidence, reason codes, summary, similar cases),
`warnings`, `requires_human_inspection: true`, a disclaimer and an `audit_id`. At least 48
intervals and one day on each side of `history_end` are required.

## 7. P4-1 literature scan and dataset choice

- Glauner, Meira, Valtchev, State & Bettinger (2017), *The challenge of non-technical loss
  detection using artificial intelligence: a survey*, IJCIS. Taxonomy of NTL methods and the
  class-imbalance / label-bias problem. Motivates precision@K and inspection-yield metrics.
- Messinis & Hatziargyriou (2018), *Review of non-technical loss detection methods*, EPSR.
  Separates data-oriented, network-oriented and hybrid methods. P4 is hybrid: energy balance plus
  consumer models.
- Jokar, Arianpoo & Leung (2016), *Electricity theft detection in AMI using customers'
  consumption patterns*, IEEE Trans. Smart Grid. Synthetic attack injection on real smart-meter
  data, plus transformer-level balance. This is the template for P4's injected theft types.
- Zheng, Yang, Niu, Dai & Zhou (2018), *Wide and deep convolutional neural networks for
  electricity-theft detection*, IEEE Trans. Ind. Informatics. Introduced the SGCC dataset.
- Buzau, Tejedor-Aguilera, Cruz-Romero & Gómez-Expósito (2019), *Detection of non-technical
  losses using smart meter data and supervised learning*, IEEE Trans. Smart Grid. Gradient
  boosting on engineered features, the same family as P4's supervised model.

Datasets chosen: **SGCC** (42,372 consumers, daily, labelled) for the supervised comparison, via
`public_datasets.load_sgcc` (not downloaded or committed); **Irish CER/ISSDA** or **London Low
Carbon London** smart-meter data for realistic base load if access is granted. All reported P4
numbers come from the P4 simulator. Running on SGCC is a remaining optional experiment.
